APPENDIX V                STABILITY SAFEGUARDS BEFORE DOCKING

V.1                        Three requirements for D/D

Stability is the most crucial requirement for getting a ship safely into a D/D. The 3 essential parameters necessary before entering the D/D are:

  • Adequate Initial G.M.: When the ship touches the blocks, there is a reaction at the point of contact that raises the center of gravity "G" and reduces the metacentric height "G.M." so that adequate initial metacentric height is necessary.
  • Upright Ship: While entering the dock, the ship must be upright: there must be no port or starboard list when it touches the blocks. The point of contact is outside the ship's centerline, which may force the ship to tip over.
  • Small or Moderate Trim Aft: The slight trim allows the stern and bow accenting in tandem rather than simultaneously, as it reduces the load and pressure on the ship's hull and the keel.

V.2                        Stability considerations

When a ship is drydocked or aground, there is a effect on stability. As the water level decreases, the keel rests on the blocks or seafloor. These objects now support a proportion of the ship's displacement. Stability is affected, like if removing weight from that point of contact. When the keel has less weight, there is a virtual rise in the ship's center of gravity.

  • As the waterline moves down, the center of buoyancy moves down.
  • As buoyancy moves down, the ship's metacenter moves up.
  • As the ship's keel rests on the blocks (a weight removal low), gravity moves up.

Since the center of gravity always rises faster than the metacenter, the two stability points eventually reach the same position. This results in neutral stability, with no creation of righting arms. The draft where G.M. = 0 is called the ship's critical draft.

Calculating the ship's critical draft is crucial. When in drydock, the dock's dewatering stops just before the critical draft, verifying that the side blocks properly support the ship. When aground, the range of tide determines if the ship reaches its critical draft.

V.3                        Critical period

The period from the critical moment when the ship's keel first touches the line of blocks and starts getting to the "sewn" on blocks position.
