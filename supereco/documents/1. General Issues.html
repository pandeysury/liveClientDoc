1.1                Purpose and Scope

The purpose of the Company's Integrated Management System (SEIMS) is to:

  • Promote Health, Safety, Security, Quality, and Environmental, including Energy Efficiency (HSQE) excellence to all organization's levels through strong leadership and a disciplined and documented management system.
  • Generate continuous and sustainable improvement in management processes through consistent measurement and feedback of the SEIMS.
  • Accomplish the stated policies and objectives to maintain the fleet management quality.

The purpose of the SEIMS Manual is to describe compliance with the following:

  • ISM Code
  • "Maritime Labour Convention (MLC 2006)"
  • "Environmental Management Systems" (ISO 14001)
  • "Energy Management Systems" (ISO 50001).

The System documentation:

  • Defines and documents the SEIMS scope, including policies, objectives, and targets.
  • Defines responsibility, authority, and interrelation of the staff who manages, performs, and verifies work that relates to and affects the HSQE management processes, and
  • Includes documented procedures and provides appropriate SEIMS references.

The overall SEIMS structure and organization relies on:

  • Appropriate and sufficient resources allocated to support the ships
  • Procedures according to National and International legislation to ensure safe ship operation and environmental protection
  • Procedures to report and analyze undesired events (nonconformities, incidents, personnel accidents, near-misses, unsafe conditions and unsafe acts)
  • Application of a condition monitoring and prevention-based maintenance system to safeguard the ship's condition
  • System performance and effectiveness evaluation through internal audits, Management reviews and Master’s reviews.
  • Integration of efficient contingency plans to respond to emergencies
  • Document and data control, including those of external origin, and record-keeping to demonstrate compliance with requirements and effective SEIMS operation.

The SEIMS boundaries and applicability consider the following:

  • External and internal issues
  • Compliance obligations
  • The Company's functions, physical boundaries and organizational units ashore and onboard
  • The Company's activities and services
  • The Company's authority and ability to exercise control and influence.

1.2                Application Field

The SEIMS applies to all the Company's activities and the following ship types:

  • OIL TANKERS
  • CHEMICAL TANKERS

The Company is responsible toward each ship's Flag Administration for the:

  • Operation
  • Technical management
  • Crewing
  • Purchase of Goods and Services.

Managed ships' ship owning companies have notified the relevant Flag Administrations that the Company is the ships' operator. The DPA and all ships keep copies of these notifications.

  • The distribution of controlled copies of the SEIMS is according to the Distribution List in the ERP.
  • The SEIMS is also available to all persons onboard and ashore in a protected electronic format in a computer network.
  • The DPA must insert the revisions in the electronically stored SEIMS Manuals and inform all interested parties accordingly.

The designated holders must receive an updated SEIMS revision, including hard copies of amendments, in a controlled (with specific identification number) flash drive. On receipt of the new flash drive, they must destroy the previous version immediately.

1.4                Working Language

The official communication language between the Company and the ships and the common working language onboard the ships is English. All procedures and instructions related to the SEIMS, external and on-board communications and, records made in the ship’s logbooks should be in the English language.

 

Warnings, identification notices ad signs posted around the ship should be in English.

The Company:

  • Is growth-oriented, focused on the operation of latest-technology ships.
  • Operates according to the highest safety and reliability standards, and promotes a company-wide quality-oriented philosophy.
  • Makes sure all ships provide customers with critical operational advantages.

Restricted Gauging System: A system employing a device, which penetrates the tank and which, when in use, permits a small quantity of cargo vapor or liquid to be exposed to the atmosphere. When not in use, the device is completely closed.

 

Resuscitator: Equipment to assist or restore the respiration of personnel overcome by gas or lack of oxygen.

Serious Near Miss (or Significant Near Miss or High-Potential Near Miss): An occurrence where life-threatening injury/illness, property, or environmental damage could have occurred if it HAD NOT BEEN for safety precautions, rescue measures, or chance. In other words, a Serious Near Miss is an event that nearly resulted in a serious marine incident.

 

Serious Illness: Medical condition of a person onboard that needs immediate medical attention.

 

Serious Incidents:

  1. Occurrences that have caused death and/or significant damages to property and/or the environment, e.g., spills/leakage/discharges creating possible health hazards.
  2. Casualties that lead to investigations by the Flag Administration and/or other formal/ governmentally authorized investigation bodies.
  3. Other incidents that rose or may raise significant attention of the mass media or may potentially have legal implications or damage the Company's reputation.

SSO (Ship Security Officer): A person appointed by the company and the ship’s Master for ensuring the security of the ship. The SSO reports to the Master for the overall management and oversight of all shipboard security policies, programs and procedures.

This is in place to protect the personnel, cargo, cargo transport units, and stores from any security related risks. The plan specifies responsibilities and procedures to counteract any anticipated threat to the ship and her cargo.

TLV (Threshold Limit Value): The TLVs refer to the maximum concentration of gases, vapors, mists, or sprays to which almost all persons onboard may be repeatedly exposed daily, without any adverse effects, assuming an 8 hours per day, 40 hours per week exposure. Due to the individual susceptibility variation, the occasional exposure of an individual to, or even below, the TLV may not prevent discomfort or aggravation of a pre-existing condition.

Top Management holds authority, resources and decision-making power regarding changes at the company.
