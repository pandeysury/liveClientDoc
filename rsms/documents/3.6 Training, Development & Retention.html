2.4        On the Job Instructions

 

Applicability:

     On the job instructions are generally be preferred when:

•      The number of trainees in any one location is small.

•      A physical skill is involved.

•      The material is difficult to communicate except by personal demonstration and participation

Instructor:

     The company (or external) instructors shall have the appropriate technical expertise in the skills being taught

Instruction Process

     As a minimum, to include:

•      Personal safety on board

•      Explaining what is to be learned.

•      Demonstrating how the task is to be performed.

•      Requiring the trainee to perform the task.

•      Providing feedback on the trainee’s performance.

•      Follow up.

2.5        Group Training

Applicability:

     Group Training are preferred when:

•      Other than a very small number of trainees are involved.

•      When the purpose is to provide technical information, solve problems or explain a method or procedure.

•      Input from several sources, including the trainee, may be required.

•      Information or skills do not have to be demonstrated at trainee’s workplace.

Instruction Process

     The instructor shall:

•      Express themselves clearly and understandably, by use of the common working language and terminology.

•      Have the technical expertise in the subject being taught.

•      Know how to use written material and audio-visual training aids.

•      Encourage trainee participation.

•      Monitor trainee performance and evaluate their progress.

•      Give trainees clear instructions and ensure that they are actively engaged in the learning process

2.6        Self-Administered Training

Applicability:

     Available for subjects considered suitable and where an extensive hand on the job experience is not required

Instruction Process

     Printed or audio-visual material is available for this type of training.

     Some objective measure of performance is provided by the trainee to demonstrate progress or proficiency in the targeted areas.

     Work instructions can also be used for self-administered training in specific work processes and progress monitored by supervisory personnel

2.7        On-board Higher Rank Training for Officers

Responsibility: Master and Chief Engineer

  • During their service on board, ensure Officers are constantly trained in higher rank duties, to prepare them:
  • To confront Emergency Situations, &
  • For promotion to higher positions.

   Also see Doc 3.5 for more details.

2.8        Training in Ship Handling

  • When circumstances allow, carry out the following:
  • Allow and guide the Chief Officer in gaining ship handling and manoeuvring experience under his supervision.
  • Advise the Chief Officer on areas of handling where there is room for improvement.
  • Provide ship-handling training to Chief Officer under different scenarios.

     e.g., making approaches to port, manoeuvring in channels, anchoring, berthing/unberthing in port and STS operations.

   Company’s training matrix requires the Chief Officer to undertake a shore based ship handling simulator course.

2.9        Training by Company staff

Responsibility: Company Training Officer or Superintendents

  • During ship visits, carry out training of shipboard personnel in Safety, Health, Environmental or any other aspect as deemed necessary.
  • Include the records of training in the attendance report.
