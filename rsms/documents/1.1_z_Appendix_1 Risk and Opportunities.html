Climate Change (emissions from vessel operations & operational impact due to climate change)

Increased scrutiny and stricter compliance requirements under IMO, EU ETS, CII ratings, and other decarbonization regulations. Rising pressure from charterers and stakeholders for sustainable operations.

 

Increased severity of extreme weather events (storms, cyclones, flooding), potential disruption to port operations, navigation safety, and supply chains.

Uncertainty remains around the timing and scope of new regulations — IMO is set to tighten CII after 2026 with additional GHG targets for 2030–2040, the EU is expanding ETS and launching FuelEU Maritime from 2025–2026, while some ports and flag states may also impose local carbon taxes or fuel restrictions, enforcement timelines, and cost impact of compliance (fuel, retrofits, carbon costs).

 

Impacting fleet schedules and operational safety.

M

Invest in energy efficiency technologies and retrofits (EEXI upgrades, hull cleaning, propeller polishing).

 

Implement and update SEEMP Plans

 

Optimize voyage planning and weather routing to reduce emissions.

 

Explore and trial alternative fuels (biofuels,) where viable.

 

Strengthen MRV/ETS monitoring and reporting systems.

 

Proactively engage with charterers on sustainability initiatives and highlight compliance as a competitive advantage.
