1.            PURPOSE & SCOPE

1.1        Purpose

  • To provide necessary instructions & guidance for:
  • Management’s Commitment towards, Health, Safety, Security, Environment and Quality through Vision, Mission Statements & policies.
  • Establishing Company Objectives and Key Performance Indicators.
  • Development, distribution, implementation & review of the SMS.
  • Ensuring that the Company Management System is suitable directed in meeting customer requirements.
  • Implementing efficient communication within the Company, & between the Company and third parties.
  • To provide information on structure of the Company Organization ashore & on board.

1.2        Scope

  • All vessels managed by the Company.
  • All shipboard and shore-based personnel.

2.            PREFACE

  • Red Sea Marine Services L.L.C (DMCC) is an independent Technical and Crew Ship Management Services for safe operations, good maintenance and provision of qualified crew, to owned and operated “Oil, Chemical and Gas Tankers” operating from in Dubai.
  • The administration and control of the Company’s SMS related activity is entirely through the Company office in Dubai. 
  • For brevity purposes “Red Sea Marine Services L.L.C (DMCC),” “the ISM Code” and “the HSSEQ Management System” are referred to, in many cases, in this manual as well as in the rest of the management system documentation as “the Company”, “the Code” and the “Management System” respectively.

3.            UNDERSTANDING THE ORGANIZATION AND ITS CONTEXT

 

  1. The Company carries out technical management and operations of following ship types:
  1. Oil Tankers
  2. Oil / Chemical Tankers
  3. Gas Tankers

 

  1. The ships trade internationally in a very competitive and highly regulated environment.

 

The ships need to comply with:

  1. International regulations (International Maritime Organization (IMO) / International
  2. Labour Organization (ILO))
  3. National regulations of the flag states
  4. National regulations of the port states
  5. Industry guidelines (example: OCIMF, BIMCO, INTERTANKO)
  6. Classification Society rules
  7.  Commercial Agreements and Contracts (example: Charter Party)
  8.  UAE federal law 24 for regional environment compliance

 

  1. Non-compliance with any of the above requirements can lead to:
  1. Detention of Ship by Port State or Flag State authorities
  2. Commercial loss due to non-performance of terms of agreement / contract
  3. Loss of reputation in market place
  4. Inability of the ship to trade

 

  1. External issues affecting the Company.

 

Following affects the Operations of the Company:

•       Changes in any of the requirements mentioned in Para B) above.

•       Changes to regulations and industry practices caused by factors such as climate, environment issues, economic conditions, and technical developments.

 

Such risks shall be evaluated as part of the Risk Assessment process and suitable action taken to manage them.

 

It is important to note following:

 

Regulated Industry:

     Maritime industry is highly regulated.

     Issues arising from legal and technological developments are applied through the Flag Administration requirements or Port State requirements.

     The Company monitors these developments and takes action, where required in consultation with the authorities or Classification Society.

Commercial operation:

     Commercial operation of the ship under management of the Company are done by ship-owner or external parties appointed by the ship-owner.

     Responsibility of the Company is to ensure ship is able to deliver the service as defined in the agreement.

Market conditions:

     The company tracks the market conditions and takes suitable measures to safeguard itself where required.

     This can include taking advance payments from the ship-owner or taking additional insurance.

Company Procedures:

     Company procedures ensure that social and cultural issues affecting human resource employed by the organization are duly addressed.

 

  1. Internal issues affecting the Company:

The specialized nature of this business requires highly motivated staff, both ashore and on ships, who are:

•       Well versed with the requirements of the operation,

•       Good team players, &

•       Able to handle tasks independently.

 

This poses a challenge for the company in an industry where there is constant inflow of newcomers due to the very nature of maritime operations in a growing organization.

 

The Company procedures for selection, recruitment, familiarization, and training of staff for shore Personnel as per SMS Chapter 2 and for Ship personnel as per Chapter 3 provide clarity on their role and responsibility.

This is to ensure that issues pertaining to knowledge, values, performance, and cultural differences are managed satisfactorily.

 

Where a particular competence required by the company is not available locally, Managing Director can consider employing external parties to provide the service based on recommendations from internal / external resources.

 

The above scenario also provides opportunities for the company to enter into new business or new markets by show casing its capabilities through consistent performance and foresight.

6.            DEFINITIONS

The following definitions are adopted from the ISM Code, ISO 9001:2015, ISO 14001:2015, and OHSAS 45001:2018.

 

.1 Acceptable Risk – risk that has been reduced to a level that can be tolerated by the Company having regard to its legal obligations and its own Health Safety Security Environment and Quality policy.

 

.2 Administration – the Government of the State whose flag the ship is entitled to fly.

 

.3 Anniversary Date – the day and month of each year that corresponds to the date of expiry of the relevant document or certificate.

 

.4 Audit – systematic, independent, and documented process for obtaining “audit evidence” and evaluating it objectively to determine the extent to which “audit criteria” are fulfilled.

 

.5 Auditor – person with the competence to conduct an audit.

 

.6 Certificate - a document issued by an Administration or its representatives that is used to show compliance with IMO requirements and used to describe operating conditions, crewing requirements, and ship equipment carriage requirements. The term "certificate" does not include publications, manuals, instructions or ships' logs used to record ongoing operations;

 

.7 Electronic Certificate - a certificate issued in an electronic format;

 

.8 Electronic Signature - data in electronic form which is attached to or logically associated with other electronic data to serve as a method of authentication of the issuer and contents of the electronic data.

 

.9 Certification – the decision by Certifying Body that the Company’s management system meets the applicable requirements of the Standard.

 

.10 Company – the Owner of the ship or any other organization or person such as the manager, or the bareboat charterer, who has assumed the responsibility for operation of the ship from the owner of the ship and who on assuming such responsibility has agreed to take over all the duties and responsibilities imposed by the ISM Code.

 

.11 Compliance Obligations – Legal requirements that an organization has to comply with and other requirements that an organization has to or chooses to comply with.

 

.12 Continual Improvement – recurring activity of enhancing the management system in order to achieve improvements in overall performance consistent with the Company’s stated policies for health, safety, quality, environment, pollution prevention, and energy, as applicable.

 

.13 Convention – means the International Convention for Safety of Life at Sea, 1974, as amended.

 

.14 Correction – action to eliminate a detected non-conformity.

 

.15 Corrective Action – action to eliminate the cause of a detected nonconformity or other undesirable situation.

 

.16 Customer – Company or person that receives a product or service. A charterer is considered a Customer.

 

.17 Customer satisfaction – perception of the degree to which the Customer’s needs and expectations have been fulfilled.

 

.18 DOC – Document of Compliance certificate.

 

.19 Document – information and its supporting medium.

 

.20 Documented Information – Information required to be controlled and maintained by an organization and the medium on which it is contained.

 

.21 Environment – surroundings in which a Company and its ships operate, including air, water, land, natural resources, flora, fauna, humans, and their interrelation.

 

.22 Environmental aspect – element of Company’s activities or products or services that can interact with the environment.

 

.23 Environmental Impact – any change to the environment, whether adverse or beneficial, wholly or partially resulting from a Company’s environmental aspects.

 

.24 Environmental Objective – objective set by the Company consistent with its HSSEQ policy.

 

.25 Environmental Management System – part of a Company’s management system used to manage environmental aspects, fulfill compliance obligations, and address risks and opportunities.

 

.26 Environmental Performance – measurable results of a Company’s management of its environmental aspects.

 

.27 Environmental Target – detailed performance requirement, quantified where practicable, applicable to the Company or its ships, that arises from the environmental objectives and that needs to be set and met in order to achieve those objectives.

 

.28 External Provider – means an external supplier to the Company (including a contractor) that provides a product or a service or performs delegated function(s).

 

.29 Hazard – source, situation or act with a potential for harm, in terms of human injury or ill health, damage to property, damage to workplace environment or a combination of these.

 

.30 Hazard Identification – process of recognizing that a hazard exists and defining its characteristics.

 

.31 Ill Health – identifiable adverse physical or mental condition arising from and/or made worse by a work activity and/or work-related situation.

 

.32 Infrastructure – system of facilities, equipment, and services needed for the operation of the Company.

 

.33 Incident – work-related event(s) in which an injury or ill health (regardless of severity) or fatality occurred or could have occurred.

 

.34 Internal Audit – systematic, independent, and documented process for obtaining audit evidence and evaluating it objectively to determine the extent to which the management system audit criteria set by the Company are fulfilled.

 

.35 Interested Parties – person or company that can affect, be affected by or perceive itself to be affected by a decision or activity. Example – Customers, communities, suppliers, regulators, non-governmental organizations, investors and employees.

 

.36 International Safety Management (ISM) Code – means the International Management Code for the Safe Operation of Ships and for Pollution Prevention as adopted by the Assembly, as may be amended by the Organization.

 

.37 Major Non-conformity – means the identifiable deviation that poses a serious threat to the safety of personnel or the ship or a serious risk to the environment that requires immediate corrective action or the lack of effective and systematic implementation of the requirement of the Code. (ISM Code)

 

.38 Non-conformity – means an observed situation where objective evidence indicates the non-fulfilment of a specified requirement. (ISM Code)

 

.39 Objective – a result to be achieved set by the Company. Objective can be strategic, tactical or operational.

 

.40 Objective Evidence – means quantitative or qualitative information, records, or statements of fact pertaining to safety or to the existence and implementation of a management system element, which is based on observation, measurement, or testing that can be verified.

.41 Observation – means a statement of fact made during a management audit and substantiated by objective evidence. (ISM Code)

 

.42 Occupational Health and Safety (OH&S) – conditions and factors that affect, or could affect, the health and safety of employees, temporary workers, contractor personnel, visitors, and any other person in the workplace.

 

.43 OH&S Management System – part of the company’s management system used to develop and implement its OH&S policy and manage its OH&S risks

 

.44 Performance – measurable results of the company’s management of its impacts and/or risks and achievement of its objectives.

 

.45 Policy – overall intentions and direction of a Company related to its performance as formally expressed by the top management.

 

.46 Preventive Action – action to eliminate the cause of a potential non-conformity or other undesirable potential situation.

 

.47 Prevention of Pollution – use of processes, practices, techniques, materials, products, services, or energy to avoid, reduce, or control (separately or in combination) the creation, emission, or discharge of any type of pollutant or waste in order to reduce adverse environmental impacts.

 

.48 Procedure – specified way to carry out an activity or a process.

 

.49 Quality Objective – Objective related to Quality.

 

.50 Record – document stating results achieved or providing evidence of activities performed.

 

.51 Requirement – a need or expectation generally stated, generally implied or obligatory

 

.52 Risk – is the effect of uncertainty, and any such uncertainty can have positive or negative effects. A positive deviation arising from a risk can provide an opportunity, but not all positive effects of risk result in opportunities. Risk is often expressed in terms of a combination of the likelihood of an occurrence of a hazardous event or exposure(s) and the severity of injury or ill health that can be caused by the event or exposure(s).

 

.53 Risks and Opportunities – potential adverse effects (threats) and potential beneficial effects (opportunities)

 

.54 Risk Assessment – overall process of evaluating the risk(s), arising from a hazard(s), taking into account the adequacy of any existing controls, and deciding whether or not the risk(s) is acceptable.

 

.55 Safety Aspect – element of a Company’s activities or services that represents an actual or potential risk to safety or personnel, or the ship.

 

.56 SMC – Safety Management Certificate.

 

.57 Success – Achievement of an objective.

 

.58 Supplier – Company or person that supplies a product or service.

 

.59 System – set of interrelated or interacting elements required to direct and control a Company.

 

.60 Top Management – person(s) or a group of people who directs or controls a Company at the highest level.

 

.61 Work Environment – set of conditions or physical location, under control of the Company, in which the work and related activities are performed.
